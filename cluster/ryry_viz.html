Step 2

The rules

A truck takes 1 to 5 addresses and is loaded to at most 60 % of its box, leaving room for safe packing with no stacking. Every stop must sit within 50 km of its group's centre. Groups ride a 1-ton truck, or a 1.4-ton when needed (at most 10 per job); bigger trucks carry one address.

Step 3

The GNN scores every pair

A graph neural network looks at each stop together with its nearest neighbours and scores how likely each pair is to share a truck. It learned this from plans on other dates, never from this one. Darker lines mean more likely.

Step 4

A short list of options

For each stop, only groups made from its 8 best-scored partners are kept. That leaves – candidate groups to choose from, instead of – when every nearby combination is tried.

Step 5

The optimizer picks the plan

An exact solver chooses the candidates that cover every stop exactly once with the fewest trucks, then the least driving. Every group is checked against the rules, so the plan can't break one. Colour shows the truck size.

Where this stands as research

A literature check found cost-of-rule studies for other rules, such as driver consistency, workload fairness and loading order. It found none that measures, with proven minimums, what these four dispatch rules cost on real bulky-delivery data. Using a neural network to shortlist options for an exact solver is established work, so the GNN is not presented as new. The check read abstracts and listings, not full papers, so it is a first pass, not a full review.

Also new since the first version

Two more methods in the comparison: agglomerative clustering, and the optimizer with each stop's 8 nearest stops. A longer search, and a proven minimum for every job. Routes shown one truck at a time, with the depot, the stop order and the end marked. Still to come: planning directly on cost, and larger simulated days. The check of the 60 % load rule against real box sizes is in version 3 below.

        The methods

        Real dispatch: the groups that were actually driven, measured on the same roads. Rule-based: the approach in Ryry's paper, biggest order first, then its nearest stops. k-means: plain geographic clustering, split wherever a rule breaks. Agglomerative: merges the closest pair by road first while the rules hold. GNN alone: joins the highest-scored pairs while the rules hold. Optimizer: an exact set-partitioning solver over every nearby combination, 60 s per job. Optimizer, 8 nearest: the same solver over each stop's 8 nearest stops, the same list size as the GNN's, to test what the GNN adds. GNN + optimizer: the same solver over the GNN's short list. Long run: lists every load a truck may carry and solves the job exactly; where a job has too many loads to list, it searches the top 12 partners plus each stop's 15 nearest for 4 minutes.

        What the GNN adds

        GNN rows are out-of-fold: each job is planned by a model trained only on other dates.

        How close to the best possible?

        Rules: 1–5 addresses per truck; load at most 60 % of the box; every stop within 50 km of its group's centre; several addresses only on a 1-ton, or a 1.4-ton (at most 10 per job); 2.5-ton and up carry one address; unlimited trucks. The real dispatch also handled things this model doesn't see, such as time windows and driver availability; its rule breaks count groups wider than 50 km, over 5 stops, or over a 1.4-ton's 60 %. Depot: Myeongseo-dong, Uichang-gu, Changwon (placed at the district's centre).
